<section id="overview">

## Overview

Section 16 is part of Workiva’s SEC filing suite. It is how officers, directors, and 10% shareholders report trades of company equity — for example, a CEO buying or selling stock.

I designed Form 4 and the surrounding filing flow for NextGen, including research, workshops, IA, wireframes, tests, prototypes, Dart implementation support, and analytics.

<Figure src="images/work/section-16/hero.webp" alt="Section 16 Form 4 in the Workiva platform" caption="Form 4, the most common Section 16 filing." />

</section>

<section id="problem">

## Keep the form they loved, fix the filing they hated

Classic users liked seeing the whole form and every table header while they added transactions. What they did not like was the rest of the process — footnotes, test file, and live file felt clunky.

How might we bring Classic’s strengths into NextGen without losing the density legal teams expect?

**Solution.** Use Workiva’s existing filing wizard, EDGARize supporting documents in-product, separate test and live file, and give footnotes a purpose-built experience with templates (most footnotes reuse language with different dates).

Success: every customer can file without Customer Success stepping in.

</section>

<section id="discovery">

## Legal users, not accountants

I started by mapping where Section 16 sits in the reporting workflow, then interviewed customers and synthesized with stakeholders.

Classic worked because users could see the entire form instead of adding transactions in a wizard or modal, read every column header, and spot editable fields. Lawyers wanted all the words on the page. Confidence in the numbers was the top priority across personas.

<section id="workflow">

## Put Form 4 in the wizard they already use

A story map showed two clear wins: attach supporting documents such as a power of attorney inside Workiva, and split test file from live file so teams could track both.

<Figure src="images/work/section-16/workflow.webp" alt="Proposed Form 4 filing workflow" caption="Proposed Form 4 workflow using the existing filing wizard." />

A more detailed journey workshop listed stories, risks, and tech spikes. Early lo-fi tests asked three questions: does the wizard feel right, can we restyle the dense tables, and should footnotes live in a panel instead of a modal?

Users loved the wizard and the separate test/live steps. They accepted a footnotes panel so they could still see the form. They rejected a prettier, less dense table. They wanted every header and every value.

</section>

<section id="form">

## Density wins, then iterate the components

High-fidelity used the current Workiva system first. Users enter from a dashboard and typically start a form for a reporting owner, so the dashboard stayed.

<Figure src="images/work/section-16/dashboard.webp" alt="Section 16 dashboard" caption="Dashboard as the starting point for a new form." />

<FigureGrid
  images={[
    { src: 'images/work/section-16/form.webp', alt: 'High-density Form 4 table' },

  caption="Table density and a right-hand panel for adding a row — users still wanted inline edits like Classic."

The wizard was a hit. The form itself did not wow anyone. Tables had too many columns, long headers, 30+ rows, and horizontal scroll tested poorly. I proposed another pass with Material-inspired components coming later in the year: denser tables, alternating rows, inline edit, and elevation to keep focus.

Footnotes moved back to a movable modal after users asked to reposition the window over the form. A live SEC preview let filers check numbers before submit.
