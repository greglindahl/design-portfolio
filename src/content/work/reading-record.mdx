## Overview

UMI was a three-person shop doing web and iOS work nights and weekends while I was still teaching. Reading Record was our product for classroom reading assessment. After three years we closed the company when I moved into a full-time UX role.

I designed the app experience, ran research, and built the public site in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

<Figure src="images/work/reading-record/hero.webp" alt="Reading Record iOS home screen" caption="Reading Record home on iPad." />

</section>

<section id="problem">

## Two fluency tests per student, per week, on paper

Teachers had to build a body of evidence for reading instruction: two one-minute fluency tests per student, twice a week, plus filing and manual records.

How might we let teachers test, track, and collect that data electronically?

**Solution.** Scan texts into the iPad and run the test there. Goals: cut admin time around prep and grading by 30%, increase one-on-one testing time, and reuse tests the following year.

</section>

<section id="design">

## Paper first, then the test flow

Sketches went in front of teachers before high-fidelity.

<Figure src="images/work/reading-record/sketch.webp" alt="Paper sketches of Reading Record" caption="Paper sketches used in early tests." />

**Administration.** Add students, group them for guided reading or RTI, and scan a page of text with the camera.

<FigureGrid
  images={[
    { src: 'images/work/reading-record/students.webp', alt: 'Student list' },
    { src: 'images/work/reading-record/options.webp', alt: 'Classroom options' },
    { src: 'images/work/reading-record/scan.webp', alt: 'Scan a text with the camera' },
  ]}
  caption="Students, groups, and scan-to-test."
/>

**Testing.** Start/stop for the interruptions that always happen. A one-minute timer replaces the stopwatch. Tap missed words, tap the last word read, and the score is calculated. History stays on the student.
